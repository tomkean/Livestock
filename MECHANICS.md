# Livestock Mechanics

What we believe about Rust's livestock breeding (added October 2026), how sure we are, and how to confirm it.
Update this file as in-game results come in; the breeding log in the app is the evidence.

Sources:
- **Video**: one YouTube video published around 2026-10-02.
- **Convars**: the descriptions printed by `find livestock` in the F1 console (test server, 2026-10-04). These come from the game itself, so they outrank the video.
- **Seen**: our own observations (see the log below).

Confidence: **Known** · **Likely** · **Guess** · **Unknown**

**Two crowding rules, confirmed 2026-10-06 - the worse one sets personal space:**
1. **Herd count within 20 m** (wiki: comfortable with up to 8 herd mates, crowded beyond 12). In the new 2-floor 5×5 farm (≈15 m across) every sheep had the whole herd within 20 m: with 36 sheep and with 16, every one showed 29-30%, in its pen, in the corridor and just outside the building. Halving the sheep per pen changed nothing. A sheep led 4 foundations (~12 m) out from the building wall rose gradually to 100% (10 foundations also worked). From there most of the 5×5 building's sheep are over 20 m away, which fits the rule: it counts sheep within 20 m of the animal, not distance from the building. So two sites only need a gap that keeps each site's sheep over 20 m from the other's: about 4 foundations between small buildings (the stat moves over time, it doesn't jump). Stacking floors doesn't help (3 m up is still within 20 m). **Confirmed by culling:** with 8 upstairs and 7 downstairs all read 30%; culling upstairs to 2 (9 in total) brought every sheep on both floors back to 100%, with no change downstairs.
2. **Close packing within ~3 m** (commit #166277): the sheep in the middle of a clump loses some (77%, 73%) even when the herd count is fine.

**Design consequence:** about 9 sheep per site (one plus 8 herd mates) at full personal space; separate sites at least 20 m apart. 9 in total is confirmed fine. **Lambs count:** the 9 adults (rams and ewes sharing rooms) bred freely, and with lambs everywhere every sheep dropped back to 30%. So any breeding site needs room under 9 for its lambs, and lambs need a verdict (cull, sell or move away) quickly, or one birth contracepts the whole site. Culling the 6 lambs (back to 9 adults) restored 100% within minutes, so the drop reverses. **Shooting lambs makes the adults around them aggressive** (2026-10-06), so culling at birth inside a harem costs a fight; leading lambs out first, or culling from outside the room, are untested alternatives. **At night sleeping adults resist leading** and break off the lead, so moves are easier by day. How personal space falls between 10 and 16 is still to measure (breeding needs 70%). The area-based rework (#166724) may replace rule 1 for penned animals when it ships.

**Overnight test, uncrowded (2026-10-06/07):** 9 adults (5 rams, 4 ewes, sexes in separate rooms, 100% personal space, troughs full) **all survived the night** and were in fair health. The earlier overnight herd starved next to full troughs while crowded, so crowding (not broken feeding) is the likely cause: keep under the herd limit and they feed themselves. In the morning every one read **83%** personal space. A uniform drop is the herd count, not the 3 m clump rule; a straight fall from 100% at 9 to 30% at 13 gives 82.5% for 10, so probably one extra animal (a wild sheep?) was in range. If so: 10 still breeds (>= 70%), 11 (~65%) doesn't. **Feed use:** about 1 barrel of food overnight for the 9, with no sign of overeating. The crowded herd went through a whole barrel (960 pumpkins) and still starved, so crowding seems to stop food doing them good rather than stop them eating. Budget about a barrel per 9 sheep per night.
**Species count separately (2026-10-07):** a cow spawned among the 9 sheep read 100% personal space, and the sheep stayed at 83% (no change). Herd mates are the same species only, so one building can hold a full sheep herd and a full cow herd (e.g. sheep in the courtyard, cows in the ring rooms).
**The 10th animal found (2026-10-07):** shooting a wild sheep just outside the old base brought its 9 from 83% to 100%. So wild sheep count as herd mates, and one animal over 9 costs about 17 points (fits 100% at 9 -> 30% by 13). **Curve (2026-10-07):** 11 or 12 sheep in one site read 65% (sheep are hard to count: they move and glitch into each other). Fits a straight fall of ~17.5 points per animal over 9: 9 = 100%, 10 = 83%, 11 = 65% (below the 70% breeding threshold), 12 = ~48% (seen: the same site flicked between 65% and 48% as one sheep, probably a wild one outside, came and went), 13+ = 30% floor. So a harem of a ram and 7 ewes (8 adults) absorbs twins (10, still breeding); with 8 ewes any twins stop breeding until a lamb is moved.
**Two sites 5 foundations apart are independent:** the new courtyard base (9 sheep) and the old base (9) both read 100%.
**Killing makes sheep hostile without them seeing it:** after shooting the wild sheep outside, the sheep inside the base turned aggressive although they couldn't have seen it. Looks like a marker on the player (like safe-zone hostility), not witnesses. So cull after any leading or moving, not before.
**Rams turn aggressive in an all-ram holding building (2026-10-07):** after 2-3 hours with no player around (and hours after any shooting), every ram in Segregation was very aggressive. Cause not yet known: stress from conditions (possible wild intruders taking it over 9), taming wearing off, or rams kept apart from ewes. Three sheep there also wouldn't offer the Lead option (wild intruders?).
**Unattended sheep wander, they don't despawn (2026-10-07):** Daryl, a ram left beside the vendor after it refused him, turned up later outside one of the harems. **Not evidence of homing:** on the test server the sheds are very near the barn, and natural barriers keep animals in about the same grid, so a straight walk from the barn gate ends up there anyway. What it does show: an unattended tamed sheep wanders off but doesn't despawn, so missing animals are more likely nearby than gone. A loose sheep near a harem counts towards that harem's 9 from outside the wall.
**Wool yield is on the card (2026-10-07):** a YIELD line under AGE: Dianna the 4th, a lamb 1 minute old with Good/Good Y (second row visible on the test server), read **16 Wool per 3 minutes** (about 320 an hour) while at 65% overall. (It doesn't: wool caps at one shear's worth, see below.) To check: the yield line for sheep with Ok and Bad Y, adults, and the same sheep at different overall condition, plus real wool collected over an hour.
**Wool rate is set by the shown Y (2026-10-07, 60 cards):** the YIELD line reads Good Y = 16 wool per 3 minutes (**320/hour**), Ok = 10 per 5 min (**120/hour**), Bad = 6 per 8 min (**45/hour**). Only the expressed (top) Y counts: Good/Good, Good/Ok and Good/Bad all read 320. **Inbred** drops it about a grade: inbred Good = 120, inbred Ok = 80/hour. The rate shown doesn't change with condition (same at 0% and 80% overall); whether actual output does is untested. A single Good Y copy is enough. **But wool stops growing at one shear's worth** (confirmed 2026-10-07): the line means '16 wool per shear, regrown 3 minutes later', not a running rate. The hourly figures are only reachable by shearing every few minutes. Realistically one round per visit: 9 Good-Y sheep give 144 wool (about 100 scrap at 400-lot prices), so wool is a bonus, not a plan - and each repeat hit while shearing risks angering the herd.
**Crowding kills fast, and blocks leading (2026-10-07):** four lambs born within ~5 minutes took Harem B past 10; within the hour animals were losing HP and an adult died (bleated, fell over; a hopper collected the body, so deaths in a building with a hopper leave no trace - possibly what happened to Rosario and Jean). While it was crowded the lambs **wouldn't accept the lead**; the moment the adult died they would, and all four were moved out. Either crowded/stressed animals can't be led, or a lamb can't be led while its mother is alive nearby (untested).
**Release instead of culling (2026-10-07):** lead an unwanted lamb away from the herd, over a barrier it won't cross by itself, and let it go. It doesn't count as a kill, so the herd doesn't turn aggressive, and once it's out of range it no longer counts towards the site's 9. The app's 'cull' verdict now says Release. **Shooting one while the herd is awake causes pandemonium:** with 9 awake the whole herd attacked; outside god mode that would likely kill the player. Shooting while they're asleep is far easier (tested 2026-10-07): some wake and turn aggressive, but there's time to kill two or three lambs before it goes crazy. So cull in small batches at night, or release instead.
Night is also a good time to **shear**: the herd is asleep and quiet. **Shearing is fiddly:** each sheep has 4 areas to shear; hitting an already-sheared area again angers the whole herd, and the wool graphics aren't reliable (a patch can look unsheared when the game counts it as done).
**Mystery cows (2026-10-07):** with one cow (Cara) and no bull, one or two more cows appeared in the base, angry. Most likely wild cows that got in through a gap; not confirmed (they were removed before being captured). Angry cows are a real hazard indoors: much bigger than sheep, they shove the player around easily.
**Night hunger (2026-10-07):** after an in-game night many sheep showed low food and some had lost HP; once awake they ate and recovered. Looks like sleeping sheep don't eat, so each night costs food and the morning is the low point. The app now keeps a conditions history per animal (Herd tab detail) to measure the overnight drop.
**Low food usually sorts itself out (2026-10-07):** sheep often let food run very low (even 0%) before eating; they only eat grass or from a trough. Leading one a few metres away can set it off eating; it nearly always resolves on its own. The app now only highlights food under 10%.
**Hotfix 7 Oct 2026 - possibly changes crowding (unconfirmed in game):** main merged `livestock_animals` (709947/709949, 15:26 UTC), which had taken in `livestock_hotfixes` on 6 Oct. If that's the shipped hotfix, it brings: the **personal space rework** (707933: penned/indoor animals get their area in m2 by navmesh flood fill and the need comes straight from area per animal - same value for all in a pen, reacts at once to building changes; free-range herds stop reproducing at a cap), **gender and familiarity shown by default** and lead break 7 -> 15 m (707953), age regression fix (708026), partial fix for cows not eating at trough/grass in a pen (708187), cow clipping (708190). Check: a ♂/♀ by the name means it's live; then re-test crowding (does a whole room share one value? how many m2 per sheep?). The 9-per-20-m rule above may no longer hold.
**Personal space changed (seen 8 Oct 2026):** after the 7 Oct hotfix, sheep in the same pen show quite different personal space, and moving one a short distance changes its value a lot. So it is now **local** (each sheep scored by how many others are close, and how close), not the old herd count within ~20 m (where a whole building shared one value) and not the area-per-animal rework described in commit 707933 (which would give a whole pen one value). The 9-per-site curve above (10 = 83%, 11 = 65%...) no longer applies; the app's headcount-from-personal-space estimate is switched off. To measure: two sheep alone in a big pen, one moved closer a foundation at a time; then three, four... and note each value.
  Three sheep close together read 23%, 42% and 65%, steady, changing only when a sheep moved: so it's set by position, not lag. The 23% one was a **lamb**; no familiarity bar on the cards. Across 272 readings since the change, lambs (<1 h) median 65% vs 83% for sheep 1-6 h old and older: lambs score ~15-18 lower. Confounded (lambs stay close to their mothers): test a lamb and an adult at the same spacing to tell position from age/familiarity.
**First god animal (8 Oct 2026, test server):** Louis, a ram lamb, Good/Good on all five genes (GGGGG / GGGGG), markers 6/4 - most likely Abigail Junior (god ewe on D L Y F, G/O on H) x Dean Junior, bred in the one-ram 'Honeymoon' room. Under a week after livestock shipped. The live-only app gave him <1%: with no parents recorded, five greens on the top row are common and say little about the copies underneath.
**The vendor won't buy a pregnant ewe (8 Oct 2026).** Sell her after she lambs.
**Familiarity / bonding gates breeding (leading explanation, 8 Oct 2026):** commit 706150 (30 Sep): "Decay livestock taming if not full bond, don't allow sleepers to tame passively, give calves a start bonus to familiarity but don't max it out" - to stop passers-by taming wild cows that then breed. With 707933 ("stop reproducing even if fully bonded"), it reads as: animals bond with players passively while a player is near and awake; only fully bonded animals breed; below full, the bond decays when no one is around. Fits: good pairs side by side that don't mate for ages (not fully bonded), a ewe pregnant within seconds of maturing (grew up bonded), and the rams in Segregation that turned aggressive and wouldn't lead after 2-3 h alone (taming decayed). Practical: stay near a breeding pair for a while; animals left alone lose the bond. 707953 shows familiarity on the card by default, but not on this server yet - look for a livestock panel convar.
**Arrivals may trigger mating (8 Oct 2026):** Louis (god ram, grown ~13:14, 96-97% overall) sat for an hour or more in Harem B with three eligible ewes (78-82% overall) and none got pregnant. Swapping rams between the harems at ~14:40: within minutes of Dale arriving in Harem B, Abigail and then Irene were pregnant. Fits either a mating check that runs when animals come together (so changing the company - a different ram, or a short walk - would help) or something about Louis (young ram, bond). Test: whether Louis breeds soon after arriving in Harem A. **Result:** Eva Junior was pregnant within minutes of Louis arriving in Harem A (the only grown ram there). So Louis is fine and **an arrival triggers mating**: a pair or harem that has settled may need its ram swapped with another harem's, or taken for a short walk, to get breeding going again.
**God pair (8 Oct 2026, test server):** Annette, a ewe born 15:08, Good/Good on all five genes, joins Louis (ram, born 12:14). From the first spawned sheep (6 Oct 14:58) about 48 hours of wall time, about 15 hours of play with Auto capture running, 65 spawned/bought, 98 bred, 79 sold. Neither god animal had its parents recorded.
**Shearing (8 Oct 2026):** harvesting tea plus bear pie made no visible difference to wool per shear (not measured closely). With the rams kept away, the ewes are much more docile while being sheared - a shearing room of ewes only (rams moved out first) avoids most of the aggression a repeat hit can set off.
**Temperament on spawning:** spawned sheep come out hostile; the one spawned cow was docile (one observation).

**Floor test (test server, 2026-10-06):** 4 sheep in a 2×2 downstairs showed personal space 100%, 100%, 100% and 77%; one sheep in the 2×2 directly above showed 100%. So personal space is **per animal, from how close the others are** (fits the radius rule; the area-based rework isn't live), closeness costs something even below the crowded count, and **the floor above didn't feel the group below**. The 77% sheep was **in the middle of the clump**; the four had bunched onto 2 of the 4 foundations, so all had the other three within ~3 m, yet only the middle one lost personal space. So it isn't a plain headcount within 3 m: either nearer neighbours cost more, or the radius is smaller (2.5 m, i.e. the 30 Sep commit is live). Rule of thumb: the tighter they bunch, the more it costs, and the sheep in the middle pays most. **They flock:** led away from the clump, the 77% sheep's personal space rose, but she walked straight back. So a pen's layout can't stop clumping; a harem is one clump. What matters is how big a clump can get before its middle sheep drops below the 70% breeding threshold: **4 gave a lowest of 77%, 6 gave 73%** (others 77% and 100%). So a harem should be a ram plus up to 5 ewes. **Spread out, 6 sheep in the same 2×2 (1-2 per foundation) all showed 100%**, even with sheep on the next foundation within 3 m: only tight clumps cost personal space. So a 1×1 stall with 2 sheep should keep both at 100% (e.g. a ram + ewe breeding stall) - if they can eat there (to test). With 6 below, the sheep upstairs still showed 100%: strong evidence floors don't count against each other (conclusive once it's checked standing directly above the clump's middle). To confirm: pack 6+ below (crowded) and check the sheep above.

**Crowding is local:** #166277 shows personal space counts animals within **3 m** (crowded 4, severe packing 6) - changed on the feature branch to 2.5 m / 5 / 9, so production is probably still 3 m / 4 / 6. Not 20 m or 200 m as some web sources say. Design for it: spread sheep out, several troughs at different points rather than one (a single trough gathers everyone), no corners to pile into; separate rooms need no gap between them.

Consequences: several of our findings may be temporary bugs (starving at troughs, wrong ages, no sex on cards). The wiki's "8 herd mates within 20 m"
is live (confirmed 2026-10-06, see the crowding rules below), alongside packing within ~3 m. After #166724, enclosed floor area per animal decides it.

- **Gestation 10 min.** Growth to adult: not published; we measured 60 min (Estella).
- **Wild animals roll each gene copy about a third each** (measured, test server 8 Oct 2026: both rows of 32 sheep that were adults when first seen, 320 copies, gave Good 31% / Ok 34% / Bad 35%; top rows 52% green). The published "Good 20%, Ok 50%, Bad 30%" doesn't fit. So a wild animal is Good/Good on a gene 1 time in 9 and shows Good 5 times in 9. These were console-spawned; tamed or bought animals on a live server could differ. The app uses equal odds (scored better against the true second rows: real Good/Goods got 0.27 on average instead of 0.18).
- Breeding: adult female near an adult male, both with overall condition ≥ 0.7 (pauses at 0.6). Overall condition = the lowest of food, water and personal space.
- Inbred animals express weaker genes but still pass on their Good copies.
- Personal space: comfortable with up to 8 herd mates within 20 m; crowded beyond 12. Grazing and trough range 25 m. Wild group size: sheep 2-6, cows 1-4. Sheep HP 150.
- Taming (trust levels): Known 5 s, Tolerated 30 s, Bonded 5 min, Herd 15 min. Hurting the animal adds 60 s, hurting its herd 30 s.
- Selling: grown animals sell at the Stable / Large Barn vendor for a **randomised offer based on gene quality**; 3 offers, then it refuses for 15 min. **Wild and purchased animals sell at a penalty; bred animals at full value.** Wool trades for rewards, up to 400 per transaction.
- Buying calves (one guide's prices; may vary by server): wild Ok 373, wild Good 582, bred Ok 466, bred Good 700 scrap. So the vendor grades what it sells.
- Milk: tame adult cow, **1 milk per milking, every 5 min** (rusthelp; Y effect not stated, ×1.6 for Good if it follows the other genes). Spoils after 48 h. A powered fridge or vending machine skims 1 milk into 1 skimmed milk + 1 cream over 4 h. **Every Milky Pure tea = 1 Pure tea + 3 milk.** Pie amounts not found. Our use: milk goes into teas and pies, never into the biofuel generator. Open: does milk build up between milkings or cap at 1 like wool?
- Biofuel generator: organic matter → low grade fuel; output by calorific value; needs stirring about every 30 min. **Dung works but gives only 1 low grade each** (worthless); **milk isn't accepted.**
- Infrastructure: Animal Fence 250 wood, Fence Gate 500 wood, Hitch & Trough 200 wood (a cow drinks up to 200 ml per session).

## Breeding conditions

| Rule | Confidence |

## Economics

**How the vendor prices (8 Oct 2026, 52 visits incl. wool):** the three ordinary offers for one sheep are worth the same total (agree within a median 15 scrap once items are valued): he sets a value, then fills each offer with random items and tops it up with scrap. Item values solved from the offers (scrap each; rough where seen rarely): metal fragments 0.21, gunpowder 0.54, HQM ore 3.1, ammo (pistol, incendiary pistol, 5.56) ~4.3 per round, rope ~1.8/ft, tarp 7, targeting computer 18, gears 26, blue keycard 38, horse saddle 45, blueprint fragment 45, CCTV camera 49, SMG body 71, rifle body 109, rifle ~230, High Caliber Revolver (the Python) ~340 (4 seen), pressure pad ~280 (1 seen). **Total value per sheep: about 180 + 32 per green on its top row** (R^2 0.32; Good copies over both rows fit no better), averaging ~270; sex makes no difference (rams 289, ewes 270); bred ~50 more than spawned/bought (307 vs 256, partly genes). The 'scrap only' comparisons earlier understate it: much of a low-scrap offer's value is in its items.

**Wool is the business, selling animals the sideline (8 Oct 2026):** this run's 63 recorded sales gave about 5,330 scrap (about 85 per sheep) plus items; 1,000 wool gave 700 scrap plus items. A sheep is one sale per trip (a truck carries at most two), needs leading or trucking to the barn and is a risk on the way; wool travels by the backpack-load (e.g. by minicopter), sells 400 per lot with no cooldown, and can't wander off. A god herd is the ideal wool farm (Y: 16 per shear every 3 min; L: longest life; H: least feed). Realistically one shearing round per visit: 10 Good-Y sheep sheared every 15-30 min give about 320-640 wool an hour. So site a farm for safety, space and feed, not for closeness to the barn; sell animals only to clear surplus.

## Overnight experiment (test server)

Started 2026-10-05 04:56 (local): one pen, **9 adults, no lambs** - rams Taylor, Steve, Milton Junior; ewes Lou, Dale, Terri, Marie, Lori, Nettie. Left unattended about 12 hours with full troughs, rams and ewes together. Questions: how many lambs, does crowding stop breeding by itself (overall < 70%), starvation and trough use, sex ratio. **Result: every sheep starved and despawned.** An entire barrel of pumpkins feeding the trough was eaten, then they starved; bodies were gone, so how many lambs were born overnight is unknown. Lessons: a herd can't be left for ~12 h on one barrel of feed; unattended breeding adds mouths; dead animals despawn, so losses can't be counted afterwards. The barrel held **960 pumpkins**: with 9 sheep over at most 12 h that is **up to ~9 pumpkins per sheep per hour** (less if overnight lambs ate too, more if it ran out early). A 20-sheep breeding setup would need ~150-180 pumpkins an hour, ~4,000 a day - far beyond a farm base. Troughs can only be a top-up; the game is probably built around grazing real grass. Next tests: does a fenced pen on grass with no trough keep sheep fed, and how much area; which trough food goes furthest.

## Goal and strategy (provisional)

- **God ram / bull:** a male that is Good/Good on all five genes. Every offspring of his expresses Good on every gene, whatever the mother carries.
- Animals are the scarce resource, so plans should **minimise purchases first and breeding cycles second**.
- On a server without `panelalleles` the hidden copy has to be inferred, by pedigree (needs both parents recorded for every birth) or by test-breeding against a female expressing Bad. On a server with it, the hidden copy is simply read off the card.
- **Inbreeding is safe for building the stud** (confirmed by Elsie junior): it only scales what the inbred animal itself expresses. A mate with different markers gives clean offspring at full production.
- Production needs condition ≥ 0.7 to keep animals breeding, so feeding and health are part of the cycle time.
- Run experiments on sheep first (half the price), assuming cows follow the same rules.
